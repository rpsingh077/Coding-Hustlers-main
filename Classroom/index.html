The digital information ecosystem has undergone a radical transformation over the past decade, driven by the explosive growth of social media platforms, instant messaging applications, and algorithm-driven content recommendation systems. While these technologies have democratized access to information and amplified diverse voices, they have simultaneously enabled the rapid, large-scale propagation of misinformation. Fake news today is no longer limited to fabricated articles; it manifests as coordinated narratives spanning text, images, videos, voice notes, and synthetic media generated by advanced AI systems. Our first research phase introduced an AI-powered, explainable fake news detection system capable of identifying deceptive textual content in real time using transformer-based language models. This system demonstrated strong performance across benchmark datasets and emphasized transparency through explainable AI mechanisms. However, it was limited to text-based analysis and English-language content, which constrained its applicability in linguistically diverse and multimedia-driven environments. The second phase of this research significantly expanded the system's scope by incorporating multimodal and multilingual capabilities, integrating image verification, speech-to-text analysis, and regional language processing. By leveraging models such as BERT, ResNet-50, Whisper, and XLM-R, the platform evolved into a more inclusive and accessible misinformation detection tool, particularly suitable for regional and low-resource contexts. Additionally, features such as timeline generation, real-time dashboards, and bias-aware modeling addressed several practical limitations of earlier approaches. Despite these advancements, a critical gap remains: most existing systems, including our prior implementations, are fundamentally reactive. They analyze misinformation only after content has been published or widely circulated. In modern misinformation campaigns—often orchestrated using bot networks, coordinated posting strategies, and AI-generated content—such delayed detection allows false narratives to influence public perception before corrective measures can be applied. Furthermore, centralized AI models face growing challenges related to data privacy, regulatory compliance, scalability, and trust. Increasingly strict data protection laws, such as GDPR and upcoming AI governance frameworks, restrict centralized data collection and opaque decision-making. Users and institutions now demand AI systems that are not only accurate but also transparent, adaptive, decentralized, and aligned with societal and legal norms. To address these challenges, this paper proposes the third and final evolution of the research: an autonomous, self-evolving, and policy-aware AI ecosystem for misinformation mitigation. Unlike previous systems that focus solely on detection, the proposed framework emphasizes early interception, continuous learning, cross-platform intelligence, and governance-aligned explainability. By integrating federated learning, reinforcement learning, large language model–based reasoning, and regulatory-aware explanation layers, the system aims to function as a proactive digital defense mechanism rather than a passive verification tool. This final phase positions fake news detection not as an isolated classification problem, but as a long-term, adaptive socio-technical challenge, requiring intelligent systems capable of evolving alongside the misinformation they seek to combat.
